### T-004 (answer; escalated, insufficient_context)

Question: E-04 keeps coming up overnight when the drive is just sat there not running anything. Measured the incoming supply at 535 V between phases. Is that the problem?

Model decision: answer; used_article_ids: KB-004, KB-034

Rejected draft (failed_check grounding, never shown):

> E-04 is a DC bus overvoltage fault that occurs when the DC bus voltage inside the VX-200 rises above 800 V DC while the drive is stopped or running at steady frequency.
>
> Your incoming supply of 535 V between phases is not the direct cause. The DC bus voltage is derived from the AC supply through the drive's rectifier. With a 400 V supply the DC bus is at about 565 V DC, so your higher incoming voltage would result in a proportionally higher DC bus voltage, but this alone would not typically cause an 800 V trip at standstill.
>
> E-04 at standstill suggests either:
> - An external voltage source is feeding back into the drive (for example from regenerative braking or another drive on the same bus)
> - A fault in the drive's power supply or DC bus circuit
>
> Since this happens overnight when nothing is running, check for:
> 1. Other equipment on the same supply that might be feeding voltage back
> 2. Any external power sources connected to the motor or drive terminals
>
> Isolate the drive, wait 5 minutes, then inspect the motor cable and the U, V, W terminals for damage or loose connections that could allow external voltage injection.

>
> To prevent this happening again, check your cabinet ventilation and filter are not blocked, and ensure the drive has 100 mm of free space above and below and 50 mm on each side for proper airflow.

> P-05 is the acceleration time from 0 Hz to the maximum frequency in P-04. If you set P-05 to 10 seconds, the drive takes 10 seconds to go from 0 Hz to whatever maximum frequency is set in P-04.
>
> If you ask for a lower speed, the time is proportional. For example, with P-04 at 50.0 Hz and P-05 at 10.0 s, going from 0 to 25 Hz takes 5 seconds.

### T-106 (answer; answered)

Question: Drive in cement plant panel trips on high temperature every afternoon. Heatsink looks very dusty. How often should we clean it and what is the fault telling us

Model decision: answer; used_article_ids: KB-030, KB-007, KB-035

Answer shown to the user:

> The fault E-07 indicates drive overtemperature at the heatsink. Dust on the heatsink fins and in the air vents reduces cooling and leads to this fault.
>
> You should clean the VX-200 every 6 months, or more often in dusty locations. A cement plant is a dusty location, so you should clean it more frequently than 6 months.
>
> The fault is telling you that the heatsink is overheating. Possible causes include:
> - The heatsink fins or air vents are blocked with dust (which matches what you see).
> - The air temperature around the drive is too high. The drive gives its full rating from −10 °C to +40 °C and must be derated between 40 °C and 50 °C.
> - The cooling fan is not running or is turning slowly.
> - Mounting clearances are too small: the drive needs 100 mm of free space above and below and 50 mm on each side.
> - The cabinet ventilation or filter is blocked.
> - The drive is running continuously close to its rated current in a warm cabinet.
>
> Start by cleaning the dusty heatsink. Before cleaning, let the heatsink cool: it can reach 90 °C in operation.
